From an X5 recording to a trajectory

The first service version processes calibrated Insta360 X5 SDK captures. It estimates the camera's IMU/body trajectory from AprilTags and inertial measurements.

1. Record supported data

  • Use unstitched dual-fisheye SDK capture at 3840 × 1920, 30 fps. Keep stream_0.h264, video_packets.csv and imu.csv.
  • Use the AprilTags from Prepare AprilTags. Print at 100% / actual size on A4, with no scaling. The 180 mm image has a 108 mm detection border; this size is already set in the service and packager.
  • Include views containing multiple markers together so their relative positions can be determined. Keep usable tags visible regularly; this version rejects visual gaps longer than 3 seconds.
  • Use recordings up to 120 seconds (at most 3600 frames). MP4/INSV exports alone do not contain the required synchronized input.

2. Include your calibration

Provide polynomial fisheye intrinsics for both lenses, the rigid camera-to-IMU transforms, and the calibrated camera/IMU time offset. Use calibration for your device and recording resolution. Another camera's calibration can produce an inaccurate trajectory even when processing succeeds.

3. Prepare the ZIP

The SDK folder must be packaged with metadata and calibration. Download the Python packager (Python 3, no additional packages). The package format is mild.x5.sdk.raw.v1. Do not include robot logs, GT, existing trajectories, or unrelated files.

python3 make_raw_package.py \
  --raw /path/to/sdk-recording \
  --calibration /path/to/calibration \
  --calibration-description "Calibration for my camera" \
  --camera-imu-td YOUR_CALIBRATED_OFFSET_SECONDS \
  --output x5-recording.zip
metadata.json
sensor.json
calibration/cam0.yaml
calibration/cam1.yaml
calibration/extrinsics.yaml
data/stream_0.h264
data/video_packets.csv
data/imu.csv

The ZIP and its expanded contents must each fit within the service’s 2 GiB application limit. Choose your ZIP and agree to data retention to upload it. The service checks required files, camera calibration, video/IMU timing and every video frame. If a check fails, the upload panel explains what to fix. Conversion starts only when all checks pass and you click Convert to trajectory.

4. Read your result

After conversion, drag the 3D trajectory to rotate it, Shift-drag to pan, or scroll to zoom. On a touch screen, use one finger to rotate and two fingers to pan or zoom. The download button becomes available when the trajectory is complete.

The download is trajectory.tum, or a ZIP containing only that file. Each row is:

timestamp tx ty tz qx qy qz qw

Positions are in meters. The quaternion describes the IMU/body orientation in the local map. Time is in seconds from the first video SDK timestamp. The world frame is a tag selected from the recording; it is not an absolute geographic or robot frame. A separate rigid calibration is needed to express the result at a tool tip.

Output is sampled at approximately 10 Hz. Processing is offline and may take several minutes. A few boundary frames may be omitted when the camera and IMU start or stop at different times; missing IMU is not fabricated.

Passing the checks confirms that the input files are readable and structurally consistent. Conversion can still fail if usable tags are absent or initialization cannot complete. A completed trajectory is an estimate, not an accuracy guarantee for your recording.

Data use

Before uploading, confirm that you are authorized to share the recording and allow the MILD / AprilVINS team to retain the input and results for research and algorithm development. Estimated trajectories are reviewed before research use and are not automatically treated as ground truth. Anyone with a result link can view its trajectory; raw recordings are not available through the public API.
